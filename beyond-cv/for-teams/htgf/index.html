A NOTE FOR THE HTGF TEAM

We’ve crossed paths before. Sort of.

I first heard about HTGF back in 2014/15 in a university course on technology-oriented entrepreneurship. We pitched medtech ideas, sketched business models, and tried to make things happen on tiny budgets. I loved it. A Business Model Canvas, a very small budget, and an almost unreasonable amount of enthusiasm.

After building a small business and finding my way into AI engineering, I’m still excited by the same question: How do we help a promising idea become something real?

01 / What it's like to build with me

Curious enough to start.
Careful enough to check.

I don't have all the answers. I like turning unclear problems into small, testable steps with the people who actually live with the result.

01 / Understand

Ask before automating.

What hurts? Who experiences it? What would make their day better? E-commerce, restaurant operations and building products taught me that the real problem is rarely the first sentence in the brief.

02 / Make

Ship something you can touch.

I learn quickly by building. A working slice, a small user journey and a thoughtful interface are more useful to me than a sprawling concept nobody has tried.

03 / Verify

Let reality argue back.

Source links, missing-data states, automated tests and human review belong in the product. Especially when working with LLMs, I want to know what broke and why.

03 / Where I came from

A few different worlds.
One useful thread.

Good software rarely starts with software alone. These experiences changed how I think about responsibility, evidence and other people's time.

◉

The engineering foundation

B.Sc. Mechanical Engineering at TU Berlin. Biomedical Engineering master's studies (102/120 credits, no degree). At TU Berlin's IWF, I worked on research, measurement and small software projects; at BIOTRONIK, on quality and hardware validation for active implants.

↗

Building a business

I founded and ran an e-commerce business. Product research, suppliers, customer questions, logistics, late surprises. It taught me that execution isn't just an idea with a nicer slide deck.

◇

Working with people

At everphone I worked across marketplaces and partners. At Transit Restaurants I coordinated busy shifts and helped teams solve problems in real time. Clear communication matters when the clock is ticking.

✳

Learning something new

In 2025–26 I completed AI & Software Engineering training, then kept building and evaluating my own tools. I bring a solid technical base and a beginner's willingness to be corrected.

What excites me? People who can see potential in an idea early — and help the people behind it actually make it happen.

That might be an investor helping a founder, or a small engineering team making a useful tool. The part I love is enabling possibility.
