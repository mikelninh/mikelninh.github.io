01 / What it's like to build with me

I like to understand it.
Then try it.

I ask questions, make a first version, test it, and try again. Ideally with the people who’ll actually use it.

01 / Understand

First, find the real problem.

E-commerce and restaurant shifts taught me how quickly a sensible plan can meet reality. I like asking what actually gets in someone’s way before suggesting a tool.

02 / Make

Make a small version.

I learn best by making things real. Give me a small problem and a person willing to try the first version; we’ll learn more than from another long document.

03 / Verify

Find what doesn’t work.

If an AI gets something wrong, I’d like to see why, fix the process and turn it into a test. I’d rather be corrected early than be confidently wrong later.

03 / Where I came from

How I ended up
doing this.

My path wasn’t a straight line into AI. I’m glad about that: running a business and working with people changed the way I approach software.

◉

The engineering foundation

B.Sc. Mechanical Engineering at TU Berlin. Biomedical Engineering master's studies. At TU Berlin's IWF, I worked on research, measurement and small software projects; at BIOTRONIK, on quality and hardware validation for active implants.

↗

Building a business

I founded and ran an e-commerce business. Product research, suppliers, customer questions, logistics, late surprises. It taught me that execution isn't just an idea with a nicer slide deck.

◇

Working with people

At everphone I worked across marketplaces and partners. At Transit Restaurants I coordinated busy shifts and helped teams solve problems in real time. Clear communication matters when the clock is ticking.

✳

Learning something new

In 2025–26 I completed AI & Software Engineering training, then kept building and evaluating my own tools. I bring a solid technical base and a beginner's willingness to be corrected.

What excites me? People who can see potential in an idea early — and help the people behind it actually make it happen.

That might be an investor helping a founder, or a small engineering team making a useful tool. The part I love is enabling possibility.
