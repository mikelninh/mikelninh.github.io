10 / REAL LIFE VEGAN

Is veganism just for the privileged?

Why the answer begins with the way you actually shop.

GOOD QUESTION · EditorialPublished 11 October 20262 minute read
€
THE GOOD QUESTION PAPER / 002
150COUNTRIES   /   MODELLING STUDY
SPRINGMANN ET AL. · 2021

A basket of premium substitutes can feel luxurious. Rice, beans and lentils are staple foods in many communities. Both statements can be true — and not equally true everywhere.

01 / THE OBJECTION

The strongest objection

Time, money, allergies, local prices, cooking facilities and culture affect real choices. A shift worker, a family on a tight budget or someone far from shops does not have the same options as a well-resourced food enthusiast.

02 / THE EVIDENCE

What the evidence actually says

Springmann and colleagues modelled nutritionally balanced, more sustainable dietary patterns across 150 countries. Compared with then-current diets, many were cheaper on average in higher-income countries but more expensive in lower-income ones. Legumes and whole grains were important ingredients in lower-cost scenarios. So neither ‘vegan is always expensive’ nor ‘vegan is automatically cheaper for everybody’ survives careful scrutiny.

Average prices do not cook dinner.

The international modelling study offers a useful perspective, not a shopping receipt for your next supermarket trip. A statistically affordable food basket may still be unrealistic without reliable access to ingredients, time to cook or equipment. It is unhelpful to dismiss these constraints as a lack of motivation. At the same time, plant-based eating is not synonymous with premium imitation meat. Rice, oats, potatoes, lentils and beans can be economical foundations depending on location and prices. One practical experiment is a short shopping list serving five repeatable meals with overlapping ingredients. Only real prices per serving can tell a household whether the suggestion fits.

03 / THE LIMITS

What remains uncertain

The study models prices and eating patterns with specific assumptions, using historical data, not today's prices in your local supermarket. Access also depends on infrastructure, time and fair wages.

04 / YOUR NEXT STEP

A useful next step

Build five affordable repeat meals with flexible ingredients. Compare cost per serving, prevent waste and support policies that make nourishing choices accessible.
