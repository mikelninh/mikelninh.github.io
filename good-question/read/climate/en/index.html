04 / THE BIG COMPARISON

Can one plate change the climate?

One famous statistic — and what it actually measures.

GOOD QUESTION · EditorialPublished 11 October 20262 minute read
25,1%
THE GOOD QUESTION PAPER / 002

‘Buy local and the steak is fine.’ Distance can matter. But so does what gets produced, and how.

01 / THE OBJECTION

The strongest objection

Agriculture is diverse. Sourcing, animal feed, energy, methane and supply chains vary dramatically. Comparing cherry-picked products or life-cycle boundaries can mislead; not every vegetable is automatically climate-friendly.

02 / THE EVIDENCE

What the evidence actually says

Scarborough and colleagues connected 55,504 UK participants’ diet records to large life-cycle datasets. The modelled vegan group had 25.1% of the greenhouse-gas impact of high meat-eaters, with a 95% uncertainty interval of 15.1–37.0%. Land use and other indicators also differed. A separate large synthesis by Poore and Nemecek found generally higher impacts for animal foods than plant substitutes, despite substantial producer-to-producer variation.

What 25.1% actually measures.

The 25.1% figure is a ratio between the modelled average footprints of two dietary groups, not the emission score of one specific dinner. Researchers linked reported diets to food-level environmental datasets. That approach is more informative than comparing two marketing slogans, but it is not a randomised experiment in which individuals were made to change diets. Climate impact is also only one outcome. Land occupation, water use and biodiversity each need their own indicators. If someone asks about local sourcing, the rigorous answer is not “origin never matters”; it is that both origin and what is produced matter, often on different scales.

✳ Are we comparing similar life-cycle boundaries and actual production methods, or cherry-picking a dramatic exception on either side?
“Where food comes from matters. What food is often matters more.

03 / THE LIMITS

What remains uncertain

These are modelled group comparisons, not a promise that an individual will cut emissions by precisely 74.9%. Water use, biodiversity and local production conditions require their own measures.

04 / YOUR NEXT STEP

A useful next step

Start with meals you repeat. Shift away from particularly emissions-intensive animal foods towards satisfying plant foods, while also buying sensibly and wasting less.
