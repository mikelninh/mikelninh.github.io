02 / THE REALITY CHECK

B12: the tiny inconvenient truth.

One vitamin. One misconception. One manageable responsibility.

GOOD QUESTION · EditorialPublished 11 October 20262 minute read
B₁₂
THE GOOD QUESTION PAPER / 002

Veganism is sometimes defended as though every plant must contain everything. It need not. Responsible food choices do not require a flawless narrative — just reliable nutrition.

01 / THE OBJECTION

The strongest objection

‘If you need a pill, the diet cannot be natural.’ That objection identifies a real gap: unfortified plant foods are not dependable sources of vitamin B12 for people. Relying on algae, fermented foods or unwashed produce is unsafe.

02 / THE EVIDENCE

What the evidence actually says

B12 matters for blood formation and nerve function. Germany’s DGE recommends reliable B12 supplementation for vegans. The US NIH identifies animal foods as natural sources and fortified foods and supplements as alternatives. Deficiency may develop gradually. Whether something feels ‘natural’ is not a medical test of whether it works.

“Natural” is not a clinical endpoint.

Vitamin B12 is a biochemical question, not a test of loyalty to a particular diet. Unfortified plant foods cannot be counted on to provide reliable B12. Some fermented foods contain traces, but variable amounts and potentially inactive analogues are not a responsible intake plan. That makes clearly labelled supplements or suitable fortified foods part of a well-considered vegan routine. Simply owning a multivitamin does not establish that intake is adequate. Someone with impaired absorption or medicines affecting B12 may need individual assessment. These distinctions matter more than arguments about whether a pill is “natural.”

03 / THE LIMITS

What remains uncertain

Dose and frequency depend on age, absorption conditions, medications and clinical findings. We deliberately do not prescribe one universal schedule.

04 / YOUR NEXT STEP

A useful next step

Make B12 routine rather than an afterthought. Check the product label; discuss dose and testing with a qualified professional if you have symptoms, uncertainty or risk factors.
