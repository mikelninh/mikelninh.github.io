07 / WHEN THE STAKES ARE HIGHER

Children, pregnancy, older age: does it still work?

No easy headline. A greater duty of care.

GOOD QUESTION · EditorialPublished 11 October 20262 minute read
♡
THE GOOD QUESTION PAPER / 002
2024DGE POSITION UPDATE
DIFFERENT GUIDANCE FOR VULNERABLE GROUPS

When a child is growing or someone needs more nutrients during pregnancy, a compassionate ideal is not enough. Practical safety has to lead.

01 / THE OBJECTION

The strongest objection

An inadequately planned vegan diet can have serious consequences during sensitive stages of life. Energy needs, individual nutrients and access to suitable food deserve close attention. These warnings are not dismissible.

02 / THE EVIDENCE

What the evidence actually says

Germany’s DGE draws a distinction in its 2024 statement between healthy adults and more vulnerable populations. For children, adolescents, pregnant or breastfeeding people and older adults, it neither broadly recommends nor opposes a vegan diet because evidence remains limited. It strongly advises qualified dietary counselling when people choose this approach. That is deliberately more nuanced than its widely quoted older 2016 position.

Life stages are not interchangeable.

Guidance for a healthy adult cannot simply be copied onto a growing child, someone who is pregnant or an older person struggling with appetite. When needs are higher or different, an inadequate intake may have greater consequences. There is no single yes-or-no answer that fits every life stage. The DGE’s updated position explicitly reflects those distinctions and places emphasis on qualified counselling. A website can help people prepare questions for a clinician, identify relevant foods and understand the reasoning behind recommendations. It cannot monitor growth, interpret blood results, or set a personalised supplementation plan.

“Compassion, here, means extra care.

03 / THE LIMITS

What remains uncertain

Research differs across life stages and individuals. A website checklist cannot replace assessment by healthcare and nutrition professionals.

04 / YOUR NEXT STEP

A useful next step

For pregnancy, breastfeeding or a child's diet, involve a qualified dietitian and medical team early. Address B12, appropriate energy intake and individual needs.
