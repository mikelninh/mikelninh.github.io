OPEN METHODS / VERSION 1.0

Make it beautiful.
Keep it honest.

Choice Lab is a research instrument, not a persuasion engine. We test whether a change genuinely helps people make informed, satisfying choices.

01 / THE HYPOTHESIS

Can changing the context change the choice?

Each participant sees one of three menus: equal-price control, a 20.8% plant-based discount, or a plant-based preselection with an easy opt-out. Food descriptions remain comparable.

02 / THE ASSIGNMENT

One person. One randomised version.

Random assignment happens after informed consent. Each browser stores its assignment and a unique trial token to discourage duplicate entries. No personal profile is collected.

03 / THE MEASURE

Measure the decision, not the declaration.

The primary outcome is the selected meal in a hypothetical web menu. Secondary measures are optional reason and confidence. This is not a sale, observed meal consumption or a measure of animal suffering.

04 / THE LIMITS

Keep the claims proportional.

Self-selection, a single simulated decision, no participant verification, and possible repeat participation across devices limit inference. We need a registered protocol, sufficient sample, real-world outcome and independent replication before policy claims.

05 / THE DATA

Store as little as possible.

With consent, our database stores the random trial token, assigned menu, selected meal, optional reason and confidence. No submitted name, email, free text or raw IP. Hosting providers may independently maintain operational access logs.

06 / THE BIGGER ROADMAP

From choices to institutions.

Validate digital findings in restaurants and cafeterias; measure satisfaction and affordability; compare lasting habits; then examine whether institutional procurement and laws improve outcomes fairly and transparently.
