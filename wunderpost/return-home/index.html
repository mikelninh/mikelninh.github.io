Return Home · Letter 001

Before doing,
be.

An obituary for your inner overachiever. Don't worry. It's a metaphor.

By Mikel·3 min read·13 October 2026
R.I.P.Here lies the belief that you must earn the right to exist.An exceptionally small funeral
A small pause for a busy mind
Text size

Before you do anything, die.

Metaphorically. We have plans.

Not you. The tiny tyrant in your head who thinks your worth is a quarterly performance target.

That one.

Give them a lovely funeral. No LinkedIn announcement required.

Every morning, they clock in early:

Better body. Better work. Better habits. Better relationships. Better at resting. (A personal favourite.)

Somewhere after the inbox is empty, the body is perfect and the future is sorted, life will finally begin.

Except the inbox has no bottom. And life isn't waiting for permission.

R.I.P.
Rest in peace.
To the part of you that thinks it has something to prove.

Now, a necessary interruption: you still have bills to pay. People to care for. Promises to keep. Dreams worth pursuing.

This isn't a pitch to abandon your responsibilities and move into a forest. (Although the trees seem to have an excellent meeting culture.)

Ambition is not the enemy.

It's what happens when you mistake ambition for a verdict on your worth.

Your work can matter deeply without deciding whether you matter.

Your rest can be rest without becoming a personal development project.

Even your meditation is allowed to be mediocre.

✳

Try something radical.

For the next twenty seconds, improve absolutely nothing.

Feel your feet. Notice the air. Hear whatever sound is here.

No enlightenment required. No gold star.

If your mind wanders, congratulations: you have a mind.

Maybe returning home is nothing grander than that.

Not discovering some flawless, permanently serene version of yourself. That's another costume to maintain.

Just meeting this ordinary, unfinished, breathing moment.

Before doing, be.
And then do the things that matter.

Make tea. Pay the rent. Call someone you love. Write that story. Start something wildly optimistic. Help someone who cannot help you back.

Be audacious. Be useful. Be gloriously imperfect.

Don't shrink your dreams. Just stop asking them to prove you're worthy of having a life.

Return home first.

Then go make a little trouble. The kind that leaves the world kinder than you found it.

Rest in peace.
Then live.

A WUNDERPOST letter. Not a prescription for a perfect life — just a small reminder that you're already in it.

A question, not homework

What might you do today if you had nothing to prove?

Write something for yourself. Your words stay on this page unless you choose to copy them. No account, no audience, no marks out of ten.

The world can wait a moment. Welcome home.
