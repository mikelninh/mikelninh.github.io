## Application questions

### 1. A project you built yourself and what made it special

**GitLaw** started from a problem I cared about: an AI answer in law is only useful if a person can verify why it should be trusted. I built the system end to end across 5,936 German federal laws — ingestion, hybrid retrieval, a 94,178-node citation graph, APIs, interface, MCP tools and evaluation. What makes it special to me is that the product treats evidence and verification as part of the UX rather than hiding uncertainty behind a fluent answer.

### 2. Why are you excited about AI — especially something most people get wrong?

I think people overfocus on the model and underfocus on the **system around the model**. The interesting part is where intelligence meets tools, data, feedback, permissions, people and real-world consequences; a slightly weaker model inside a well-designed workflow can be much more valuable than a brilliant model inside a demo. I am especially excited by AI becoming more embodied and agentic — perception, action and learning loops — but that makes evaluation and responsibility more important, not less.

### 3. A full-stack project where you took technical leadership

On **GitLaw**, I owned the technical direction from data ingestion through retrieval, API contracts, frontend workflows, MCP integration, evaluation and deployment. I had to decide where deterministic verification mattered more than another model call, how evidence should travel through the stack and what needed to be tested before users could trust it. It was not leadership of a large enterprise engineering team, and I would not present it as that; it was genuine end-to-end technical ownership of a substantial product.

### 4. A clever non-technical solution or "hack" you used to gain an advantage

When I care about an opportunity, I try to replace **"believe my claims" with "inspect how I work."** Instead of only tailoring a CV, I build a small proof around the company's actual problem, expose the assumptions and let the other side judge the work directly. This page is an example: it costs more effort than sending another generic application, but it creates a much higher-bandwidth conversation.
