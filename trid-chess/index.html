SYSTEM REFERENCE

What makes Tri-D chess actually different?

Star Trek showed the prop, not a canonical rules document. This prototype follows the community Federation Revised Standard 5.0 interpretation and labels its remaining limitations rather than pretending the fiction supplied an official rulebook.

01

Board geometry

Three fixed 4×4 boards sit at elevations 2, 4 and 6. Four 2×2 attack boards add the remaining 16 squares.

02

Projected movement

Look down through the stack. Rooks, bishops, queens, kings, knights and pawns keep their familiar top-down movement shape.

03

Highest path

Sliding pieces travel over the highest available squares between start and target. A nearby attack board may create one alternate Path B.

04

Attack-board turns

Instead of moving a piece, move an owned attack board if it is empty or contains only one of your pawns — and you still have a pawn somewhere in play.

05

Warp-speed pawns

A pawn can ride an attack board toward promotion. Moving the board counts as moving the pawn.

06

King safety

Piece and attack-board actions are filtered if they expose your king. Kings are checkmated, not captured.

07

Special moves

Tri-D king-side/queen-side castling and standard en passant are implemented. The optional sideways rook-pawn rule can be toggled.

08

Current beta edge

Rare rook-pawn en-passant edge cases and exhaustive conformance against every published sample position still need dedicated tests.

09

Why deterministic?

The legality engine contains no LLM. Rules are explicit and testable; AI can explain a move later, but it should not invent whether a move is legal.

Built because a job ad made me curious. Vanilla JS rules engine · C#/.NET companion API · SQLite · no LLM required for legality
