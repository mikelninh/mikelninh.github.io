An applicant's plan · Not a description of INCA's internal processes

If I joined INCA,
I'd start by listening.

Before adding another agent, model or dashboard, I'd want to understand how the claims team actually works, what slows people down, and which errors matter most to customers.

WEEK 01 · LEARN

Shadow the real work

Talk with Claims Experts and observe the path from incoming evidence to a clear, human-controlled next action.

  • Map recurring exceptions and handoffs
  • Learn what evidence makes a decision trustworthy
  • Identify what must never be automated
WEEK 02 · MEASURE

Establish a baseline

Choose one narrow workflow and record what happens before introducing AI. Work only with approved, appropriately protected case material.

  • Time to find missing information
  • Reviewer corrections and critical misses
  • Cost and time per correctly handled case
WEEKS 03–04 · TEST

Run one safe pilot

Build a small improvement with the domain team and compare it against the existing workflow in shadow mode.

  • Ground suggestions in source evidence
  • Keep human approval for sensitive steps
  • Make every correction a future test case
Questions I'd ask the team

What matters most in your work?

Which handoff or missing-document question repeatedly costs your team time?
What are the mistakes that would most harm a customer or force a case to be reopened?
When do Claims Experts trust an AI suggestion — and what evidence do they need to verify it?
Which existing Claude workflows require the most corrections or context tuning as cases and model versions change?
Which tasks are safe to automate, and which must always remain under explicit human control?
Three testable ideas · Not unverified production promises

I'd test improvements, not technologies for their own sake.

Small experimentWhat we'd compareWhat success should mean
01 · Missing-evidence assistantCurrent intake vs a source-backed assistant proposing exactly which document or clarification is needed.Less time to a complete case, without increasing missed required evidence or customer back-and-forth.
02 · Better human handoffsCurrent case summary vs structured facts, exact citations, uncertainty and one proposed question for the reviewer.Faster, more accurate specialist review with fewer corrections and no unauthorized actions.
03 · Selective quality auditsMandatory code-based checks alone vs code plus Jev or GPT ranking of *optional* additional reviews.More critical issues found at a fixed review budget, and lower total cost including human effort. If rules win, keep rules.
How we'd keep it safe: define success with the Claims Experts, validate on approved or fictional cases first, measure actual handling time and failures, and never let model confidence replace source verification or human coverage/payment authority. I'd rather ship one reliable workflow than five impressive demos.
