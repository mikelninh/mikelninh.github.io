Four rules

What the system refuses to blur.

A useful clinical assistant should make these states easier to see, not harder.

01

A pending result stays pending.

“Not back yet” never becomes “negative” or “normal.”

02

If a source is down, say so.

Unavailable information is never silently treated as absent.

03

Charted treatment is not AI advice.

CareOS can show what is documented without pretending the model prescribed it.

04

An AI draft stays a draft.

Consequential work stays reviewable until a human explicitly approves it.

Why this problem

The cost of bad clinical software shows up after the shift ends.

My partner works as a physician in a Berlin hospital. When fragmented systems and documentation consume her evening, the product metric becomes concrete: good software should return that time to patients — and to her life.

CareOS is my independent attempt to understand that boundary deeply. This case asks one narrow question: can we prepare useful work while preserving source links, pending work, contradictions and human control?
