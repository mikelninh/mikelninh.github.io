The tournament commandments

Position is power. Play more hands late, fewer hands early.
Enter pots aggressively. Open-raise or fold far more often than you limp.
Bluff fewer people. Multiway pots and calling stations are value territory.
Short stack = first-in aggression. With 10 big blinds, shoving is often cleaner than calling.
One pair is not a marriage. You are allowed to fold top pair.

Pot odds in plain English

Required equity = call ÷ final pot after you call.

Pot €80, opponent bets €20. You call €20 to play for €120. Required equity = 20/120 = 16.7%.
Rule of 2 and 4: outs × 2 with one card to come; outs × 4 with two cards to come.

Common draws: flush draw = 9 outs; open-ended straight draw = 8; gutshot = 4; two overcards ≈ 6, though not all outs are always clean.

Stack-size plan

40bb+: manoeuvre, apply pressure, avoid massive pots with medium hands.

20–40bb: steals and re-shoves become powerful; speculative calls lose value.

10–20bb: fewer flat calls, more raise/fold and shove decisions.

Under 10bb: protect fold equity. Do not wait for aces until the blinds eat your soul.

Exploit your friends lovingly

Calls too much: bluff less, value bet bigger.

Folds too much: steal blinds relentlessly.

Raises only monsters: believe them.

Tilting: stay patient and let them donate voluntarily.

Looks at chips before acting: often indicates interest in betting—but never rely on one tell alone.

Before the tournament starts

Agree starting stack, blind levels, rebuy rules, breaks, late registration, chip denominations, and whether verbal declarations are binding.

Avoid string bets. Say “raise” and the amount before moving chips.
Protect your cards. Keep them visible and use a chip/card protector so they are not accidentally mucked.
Count in big blinds. “I have 14bb” is strategically useful; “I have 7,350 chips” mostly sounds impressive.
Strategy answers use solid default tournament play. Close spots can vary with antes, player tendencies, payout structure, and table size.
